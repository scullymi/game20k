Flashing the {project} {version} firmware onto a BL616

Pick the image for your board. Each comes with its NOTICE and a configuration for
Bouffalo's flash tool:

  {project}-{version}-nano20k_v3923.bin  Tang Nano 20K of board revision 3923, alone
  {project}-{version}-nano20k.bin        Tang Nano 20K of board revision 3921, alone
  {project}-{version}-m0sdock.bin        Sipeed M0S Dock on the header of a Tang Nano 20K

The revision is printed on the underside of the Nano. The two revisions wire its
BL616 differently, so the wrong image does not work. The cores of the same version
go onto the Nano's FPGA:
{project}-{version}-cores-tangnano20k-singleboard.zip for the Nano alone,
{project}-{version}-cores-tangnano20k.zip for the M0S Dock.

The flash tool is BLFlashCommand (BLFlashCommand-macos, -ubuntu or .exe), or the
graphical BLFlashCube, both in tools/bflb_tools/bouffalo_flash_cube of the Bouffalo
SDK: https://github.com/bouffalolab/bouffalo_sdk

Tang Nano 20K: the image goes to 0x40000, behind Sipeed's debugger firmware at 0x0,
which has to stay. flash-nano20k_v3923.ini and flash-nano20k.ini write only that
address. Unplug the board, hold UPDATE, plug it in, then run in this folder:

  BLFlashCommand --interface=uart --chipname=bl616 --baudrate=2000000 --port=<port> --config=flash-nano20k_v3923.ini

The BL616 starts the Companion only when no computer is on the board's USB port, so
power the board from a power supply or a powered hub to play.

M0S Dock: the image goes to 0x0. Unplug the dock, hold BOOT, plug it in, then:

  BLFlashCommand --interface=uart --chipname=bl616 --baudrate=2000000 --port=<port> --config=flash-m0sdock.ini

The port is e.g. /dev/ttyACM0 on Linux, /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 on macOS or COM3 on
Windows. More in docs/bl616.md of the repository:
https://github.com/scullymi/game20k/blob/v{version}/docs/bl616.md
